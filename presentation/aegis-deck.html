02

The problem

  • Nobody knows the number. In an emergency a student opens their contacts and hunts for a warden's number they never saved.
  • The message loses the details. A phone call gives no record of which block, which floor, or what was said.
  • Everything is equally urgent. A sprained ankle and someone who has stopped breathing arrive on the same WhatsApp group, in the same font.
  • No one owns it. If the first person paged is teaching, in a meeting, or asleep, nothing escalates — the report just sits there.
  • The reporter is left blind. They have no idea whether help is coming, so they call again, and again.

ASMT operates across three blocks. Main, Academic and Hostel — a responder on the wrong side of campus is minutes away from someone who may have seconds.

Aegis targets the gap between something has happened and the right person knows, and has said they are coming.

Two-stage classification

Our differentiator

An emergency system that stops working when an API key expires is not an emergency system. So the AI never sits on the critical path.

  • Stage 1 — rule engine. A deterministic keyword scan that always runs and cannot fail. English, Hindi and romanised Hinglish.
  • Stage 2 — AI second opinion. One prompt to Claude or GPT under a hard 4-second timeout, swappable with one env var.
  • The more severe verdict wins. AI can escalate a report; it can never talk the system down from a rule-matched emergency.

During the build our OpenAI key ran out of credit mid-demo. The alert still went out, correctly marked Critical — by design, not by luck.

Live output, real report
description  "Student collapsed near the
              stairs and is not breathing"

rule_priority   critical
rule_matched    ["not breathing", "collapsed"]
ai_priority     null        (429 no credit)
final_priority  critical    <- rules carried it
telegram        delivered

--- Hindi, same pipeline ---
"छात्र बेहोश है और खून बह रहा है"
rule_priority   critical
final_priority  critical

A latent bug we caught here: the text normaliser stripped every non-ASCII character, which silently blanked Devanagari and would have scored a real emergency as normal.

Nobody answered. Now what?

Most systems stop at "notification sent". Aegis treats silence as a failure state.

TierPagedTriggerRecorded
Tier 1Medical + Security On classificationescalations row + timeline
Tier 2Security + Warden 60s unacknowledged (critical)Re-notified on Telegram
Tier 3Warden + Admin A further threshold passesFull audit trail
  • Thresholds scale with severity — 60s critical, 180s urgent, 600s normal.
  • Idempotent by construction. Each tier is paged exactly once, enforced by the escalations table, so five open dashboards cannot page a warden five times.
  • The countdown is visible on every card — "Escalates in 23s" — so responders can see the clock they are racing.
  • Missing the deadline sounds an alarm on the dashboard, not just a silent database row.
08

Built for everyone on campus

  • Hindi, spoken and understood. Dictate the emergency in Hindi and hear status updates read back in Hindi. The classifier reads Devanagari and Hinglish, so "बेहोश" and "behosh" both trigger critical.
  • For blind users. Voice announcements read every status change aloud, with aria-live regions for screen readers and accessible names that match their visible labels so voice control can reach them.
  • Hands-free reporting. Speak instead of typing — for someone who cannot see the screen, and for anyone whose hands are dealing with the emergency.
  • Larger text and high contrast, saved per device with no account.
  • Lighthouse accessibility 100. We re-enabled pinch-zoom after measuring that blocking it was the only thing keeping the score below 100.

Our chart palette was validated programmatically: green and amber separate by only ΔE 7 under protanopia, so every bar carries its label and count as text. Colour is never the only signal.

When things go wrong

Failure is the normal case
No network
Offline outbox
A failed report is written to IndexedDB and sends itself when signal returns. The user sees "Saved — will send when online".
No data at all
Voice and SMS fallback
A permanent Call Security bar and a pre-filled emergency SMS, both of which work on cellular voice with zero data.
Websocket blocked
Triple-redundant live sync
Realtime, plus a resync on every reconnect, plus polling. The screen cannot silently freeze.
AI provider down
Rule engine carries it
Classification never depends on a third party being up.
Five reports, one fire
Duplicate grouping
Same type, same block, inside 10 minutes folds into the original — one alert, shown as "Confirmed by 3 people".
False alarm
10-second undo
The reporter can withdraw immediately, until a responder acts on it.
10

Safe Walk

Silence as the alarm

Every other feature assumes the person can ask for help. This one assumes they cannot.

  • A student walking back to the hostel alone starts a timer — 5 to 30 minutes — before setting off.
  • Arriving safely takes one tap.
  • If the timer expires with no check-in, that silence raises a critical security incident automatically, at their last known GPS position, and pages tier 1.
  • The walker's own device triggers the sweep the moment its timer runs out, so the alert does not wait for anyone to be watching a dashboard.

The report is raised pre-classified as critical — a missed check-in never waits on a classifier or an AI call.

11

The responder side

  • Live incident board, sorted by priority then by who has waited longest. Critical cards flash and sound a looping alarm until acknowledged.
  • Station-wide alerts. A signed-in responder on any screen gets a takeover sheet, an alarm and an OS notification — one tap to the incident.
  • Acknowledge sends their position, and the reporter immediately sees a real ETA instead of an open-ended "on the way".
  • Campus schematic showing all three blocks with live incident dots, coloured by severity.
  • QR generator — print every location code, four to a page.
  • Analytics — median acknowledgement and resolution time, by block, type and hour of day.
  • Two ways in: Supabase magic-link email, plus a staff PIN, because free-tier auth mail is rate-limited and a dashboard you cannot open during an emergency is worthless.

How it fits together

QR code  ──►  /report?campus=…&location=…
                 │  anonymous insert, enforced by Row Level Security
                 ▼
            incidents ──► /api/classify ──► rule engine   (always runs)
                 │                     └──► LLM           (4s timeout)
                 │                               │
                 │                    final = MAX(rule, ai)
                 │                               │
                 │                               ├──► duplicate grouping
                 │                               ├──► Telegram Bot API
                 │                               └──► incident_events
                 ▼
         Supabase Realtime ──► reporter's live status page
                            └──► responder dashboard ──► /api/escalate
                                                    └──► /api/safe-walk/sweep
  • 6 tables — campuses, locations, responders, incidents, incident_events, escalations — plus push subscriptions and safe walks.
  • Every state change is an event row, so each incident has a complete, timestamped audit trail rather than just a current status.
  • Realtime on incidents and events, with replica identity full so update payloads carry the whole row.
  • 11 pages, 10 API routes, first-load JS under 100 kB on the public screens.

Security model

Reporting must be open to anyone on campus. Acting on a report must not be.

TableAnonymousSigned-in staffWhy
campuses, locationsreadread A QR scan must work with no login
incidentsinsert + readread + update Anyone may report; only staff may resolve
incident_eventsinsert + readfull The reporter follows their own timeline
respondersno accessread Holds staff phone numbers and Telegram IDs
  • Responder contact details are the one thing anonymous users cannot read — verified by querying the live API with the anon key and confirming it returns empty.
  • The service-role key never reaches the browser. A server-only import turns any accidental client import into a build error.
  • Staff actions run server-side behind an HMAC-signed, httpOnly session cookie with constant-time PIN comparison and brute-force throttling.
  • Anonymous reporting is a feature, not a gap — harassment can be reported without giving a name, while responders still get the exact location.

Two platforms, one app

Tested on both, not assumed
iOS quirks we handled
  • No Vibration API at all. SOS confirmation falls back to a visual pulse and a WebAudio beep.
  • Audio is locked until a user gesture. The audio context is created inside the tap handler, and re-resumed after every screen lock.
  • 100vh is wrong on Safari — we use 100dvh with a -webkit-fill-available fallback.
  • Inputs under 16px zoom the viewport, so nothing is smaller.
Android + shared
  • Vibration used where it exists, including an SOS pattern on critical notifications.
  • Safe-area insets for the notch and the gesture bar, applied once as tokens rather than per screen.
  • Native selects, so each OS renders its own picker.
  • Maskable icon inset to 72% so Android's circle crop never clips it.

Honest limitation: a web page cannot place a phone call without the OS confirming it, on either platform. We removed every extra step on our side — Call Security is one tap to the dialer — but that final prompt is a browser security guarantee, not something we can or should bypass.

17

Where it goes next

Known limitations
  • Escalation runs from an open dashboard. Vercel's free tier caps cron at once per day; the cron is declared and a paid plan moves both sweeps server-side unchanged.
  • The staff PIN is shared. Magic-link auth is built and works; the PIN exists because free-tier auth mail is rate-limited.
  • The campus map is a schematic, not a real map — three adjacent buildings read more clearly as a diagram, and it costs no network request.
Next
  • Web Push so a warden is paged at 2am with the app closed.
  • Live responder tracking — a moving ETA rather than one taken at the moment of acknowledgement.
  • Integration with the college gate and CCTV, so a critical incident can surface the nearest camera.
  • Drill mode — run a fire drill through the real system and measure the response, turning the audit trail into training data.
